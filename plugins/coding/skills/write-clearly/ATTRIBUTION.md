# Attribution

This skill synthesizes and paraphrases ideas from:

- George Orwell, ["Politics and the English Language"](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/)
  (1946): stale figures of speech, short and familiar words, deletion, active
  voice, jargon, euphemism, and the rule-breaking exception. The essay remains
  under copyright in some jurisdictions; no passage is reproduced here.
- William Strunk Jr., [*The Elements of Style*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/37134)
  (1918): paragraph unity, concrete language, concision, parallel construction,
  proximity of related words, and purposeful emphasis. This edition is in the
  U.S. public domain.
- U.S. General Services Administration,
  [Plain Language Guide Series](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language):
  audience-first wording, organization, strong verbs, scannable design, and
  reader testing.
- UK Government Digital Service,
  ["Use clear language"](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
  and
  ["GOV.UK content principles"](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background):
  plain English for specialists, contextual use of technical terms and passive
  voice, and front-loaded web content.
